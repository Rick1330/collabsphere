## §1.3 Problem Statement

### For Professional Teams

Software development teams currently operate across a fragmented ecosystem of tools. Documentation lives in Notion or Confluence, tasks live in Jira or Linear, discussions happen in Slack, and file sharing happens in Google Drive. This fragmentation creates several critical problems:

- **Context switching overhead.** Engineers spend 20-30% of their time navigating between tools rather than doing productive work.
- **Lost context.** A decision made in Slack is disconnected from the document it relates to and the task it impacts. Project history becomes scattered and unreconstructible.
- **Onboarding friction.** New team members must learn 4-6 different tools and piece together project context from multiple sources.
- **Inconsistent workflows.** Each tool has its own notification system, permission model, and organizational structure, leading to information gaps.

### For Academic Teams

University project groups face the same fragmentation at a smaller scale but with additional academic-specific challenges:

- **Tool chaos.** Students cobble together Telegram for chat, Google Docs for writing, Trello for tasks, and email for file sharing. There is no single source of truth.
- **Version control nightmares.** Without integrated collaboration, teams produce files named `Final_v2_REAL_FINAL.docx`, losing track of the authoritative version.
- **Supervisor blind spots.** Faculty advisors have no visibility into team progress without scheduling manual status meetings. They cannot see who is contributing, what is stalled, or whether deadlines are at risk.
- **No submission workflow.** Submitting work for review involves emailing ZIP files or sharing Google Drive links, with no structured feedback loop.
- **Free-rider problem.** There is no audit trail of individual contributions, making it difficult to assess participation fairly.
